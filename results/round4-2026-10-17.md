## New findings

### 1. Windows grow 1.25× at each rebuild (seen by the Mac)
- The front window on the PC grew from about 1592x903 to 1992x1130 at R1 (**exactly 1.25×**, the text the same size), then to almost the full screen at R2.
- The trace shows each rebuild leaving **no virtual display for about half a second**:
  - R1: `DisplayRemoved` 06:40:40.023, then `DisplayAdded` 06:40:40.507;
  - R2: `DisplayRemoved` 06:41:33.845, then `DisplayAdded` 06:41:34.359.
- The virtual display's scale didn't change. Windows' saved scale for it is one value throughout: `DpiValue` −3, i.e. 125% (200% recommended, minus 3 steps).
- **The likely mechanism** (not proven: the 2 s sampler can't see a 0.5 s gap):
  1. While the virtual display is gone, Windows moves its windows to a physical monitor at 100%.
  2. When the new display appears at 125%, they come back scaled by **125/100 = 1.25**.
  3. Two rebuilds give 1.25² ≈ 1.56×, which is "almost full screen".
- **Supporting evidence:** right after L1's quit, the sampler saw **only the Optix G24C active** (1920x1080 primary at 0,0) for about 6 s, before both monitors returned.
- **Suggestion:** keep a display active during a rebuild, for example by adding the new display before removing the old one, or restore window placements after it.

### 2. R2 had no audio on the Mac
- The Mac logged `No audio traffic was ever received from the host!`. Every other stream, R1 included, got audio by about +5 s.
- **Apollo's log shows R2's audio starting normally:**
  - `Selected audio sink: virtual-Stereo…` at 06:41:38.306;
  - `Changed virtual audio sink format to [S24 48000 2.0]`;
  - `Audio capture format is [F32 48000 2.0]` at 06:41:38.386.
- Apollo logged no audio error or warning, so nothing on the PC side explains it at Info level. It was the second rebuild in a row. Worth a debug-level look at the audio sender after a rebuild.

### 3. R2's mid-stream overflow on the Mac
- On the Mac at 06:41:52: `Video decode unit queue overflow`, then an IDR request.
- Apollo:
  - R2's `over 17.0 s` line counts 3 keyframe requests, like every stream (2 at start, 1 later). The third falls between 06:41:48 and 06:41:54.9, consistent with the Mac's 06:41:52 request.
  - 0 frames lost or repaired, so the network didn't drop anything.
  - Nothing else was logged between 06:41:46 and the disconnect.
  - R2's two capture restarts came 6–8 s earlier (06:41:43.6 and 06:41:45.5), not at the same time.
